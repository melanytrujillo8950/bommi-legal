Community Guidelines

bommi is for meeting people through what you both like doing. These are the rules that make that possible. They're short on purpose: if you have to read them twice to know whether something is allowed, they're badly written.

Last reviewed: 18 September 2026

Privacy Terms Guidelines Español

👤 Who can be here

  • 18 and over only. Your date of birth is asked for when you join, and lying about your age is grounds for closing the account without warning.
  • One username per person. No second accounts, and no pretending to be someone else.
  • Your photos must be yours, recent, and your face must be visible.

💚 How people get treated

  • No insults, threats or mockery, in chats or in person.
  • No sexual messages or photos to anyone who didn't ask for them.
  • If someone says no, you don't push. That's harassment.
  • No discrimination over skin colour, religion, nationality, orientation, gender identity, disability or age.

📝 What you put on your profile

  • What you say you like doing has to be true. This is not a place to advertise anything.
  • No selling, charging or promoting anything.
  • No plans that involve anything illegal. Having a few drinks can be talked about — that's what nightlife is for — drugs can't.
  • The app never says where you are: only how many kilometres away. You give the meeting point in the chat.

🚶 When you meet in person

  • The first time, somewhere public.
  • Tell someone you trust where you're going and who with.
  • You can leave whenever you want, without explaining yourself. You don't owe anyone an explanation.
  • Verification says a person is who they say they are. It doesn't say they're a good person. Keep your head on.

⚠️ What is never okay

  • Asking for money. Not a loan, not an emergency, not an investment. Never.
  • Pulling someone off the app after two messages to keep talking somewhere else.
  • Photos or videos of minors, sexual content, violence or drugs.
  • Sharing someone else's photos, messages or screenshots outside the app.

🚩 What happens when someone breaks these

  • You can report anyone from their profile or from the chat. Reports are anonymous: the person is never told they were reported, or by whom.
  • You can block anyone. The person you block gets no notice at all: you simply stop seeing each other.
  • Depending on what happened, it may end in nothing, in a warning, or in the account being closed. What decides it isn't how many reports someone has, but what was reported and what we found when we looked.
  • Reporting someone doesn't take anything away on its own, and neither does blocking. They're two different things and you can do both.

If something isn't on this list but made you feel bad, report it anyway. The list is short because it has to be readable, not because it's everything that matters.
